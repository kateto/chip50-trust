The trust question changed wording in 2022. From April 2020 to September 2022 (W1 to W24), CHIP50 asked: “How much do you trust the following people and organizations to do the right thing to best handle the current coronavirus (COVID-19) outbreak?” Since October 2022 (W25 on) it asks how much people trust them “to do what is right.” The answer options stayed the same: a lot, some, not too much, not at all.

Why it matters. The earlier question is about the pandemic, so its answers are not directly comparable with the later, general question. Trust in public health agencies, for example, partly reflected how people judged the pandemic response. The page therefore never joins the two into one line: the COVID-19 wording is drawn as a dashed line, the current wording as a solid line, and a marker shows where the wording changed. In June to August 2022 (W23) the two wordings were asked side by side, each to about half of the sample, so that survey shows how much the wording itself moves the answers; the next survey (W24) used the COVID-19 wording only. For a survey period from before June 2022, the COVID-19 wording is the only one there is.

The trust map

Institutions close together are trusted by the same people: someone who trusts one tends to trust the other. Circle size shows how widely each is trusted. Lines join pairs with a correlation of 0.6 or more. Hover over an institution to bring its links forward; drag one to move it, and it stays where you drop it (double-click it to let it go back). The map is turned to line up with the latest year, so positions can be compared across periods; it settles for a few seconds so that names do not overlap, and institutions with few or no links drift toward the edge.

Every estimate is a weighted percentage from one survey wave (or a pool of waves), with its margin of error and the number of respondents behind it. Estimates with an effective sample (after weighting) below are left out.

The columns

The CHIP50 Trust Tracker shows how much Americans trust 33 institutions and public figures, from CHIP50 surveys of adults in all 50 states and DC, April 2020 to November 2026: city government, state government, Congress, the White House, the U.S. Supreme Court, the U.S. election system, the FBI, the CDC, the FDA, hospitals and doctors, scientists and researchers, pharmaceutical companies, colleges and universities, the police, the military, the criminal justice system, organized religion, banks, the news media, social media companies, AI companies, CNN, Fox News, the New York Times, Google, Facebook, Twitter, Donald Trump, Joe Biden, Kamala Harris, Dr. Anthony Fauci, Elon Musk and Robert F. Kennedy Jr. Weighted estimates can be broken down by gender, age, race and ethnicity, education, party, ideology, household income, community type, region and state, compared over time, and downloaded.
